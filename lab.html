Understanding compounding

Personalized medications, carefully prepared.

Sterile Compounding

Sterile compounding involves preparing medications that must be completely free of bacteria and other contaminants.

These medications are compounded in highly controlled cleanrooms using specialized equipment and rigorous quality standards because they are administered directly into the body.

Common examples include injectable medications, IV infusions, ophthalmic (eye) medications, and other sterile therapies.

Non-Sterile Compounding

Non-sterile compounding involves creating personalized medications that do not require a sterile environment.

Pharmacists can customize the strength, dosage form, ingredients, or flavor of a medication to meet an individual patient's needs.

Common examples include capsules, oral liquids, creams, gels, troches, suppositories, and topical solutions.

Why Patients Choose Compounding

  • Personalized strengths

How quality is measured

3 Essential Measures of Quality

Quality compounded medications are built on three essential measures: sterility, potency, and stability. Together, they help ensure every formulation is prepared safely, accurately, and consistently.

  1. Sterility

    Sterility means a medication is free from harmful bacteria, fungi, and other microorganisms. This is especially important for sterile preparations such as injections, IV infusions, and ophthalmic (eye) medications, where patient safety depends on a contaminant-free product.

  2. Potency

    Potency confirms that a medication contains the correct amount of active ingredient. Every formulation is tested to help ensure patients receive the prescribed strength their healthcare provider intended.

  3. Stability

    Stability evaluates how well a medication maintains its strength, quality, and effectiveness over time. This testing helps ensure compounded medications continue to perform as intended throughout their expected shelf life.
